Why this exists

I loved Wispr Flow. I just didn't want to pay for it.

  • ✕Local open-source apps: they run the model on your Mac and eat RAM, battery and fans.
  • ✕A monthly subscription for something I suspected I could build myself.
  • ✓Cloud transcription that's fast, cheap, and runs on a key I control.

Wispr Flow changed how I write. You hold a key, talk the way you think, and polished text appears in whatever app you're in. But in the back of my head I kept thinking: this is achievable. This can be coded.

There are open-source dictation apps, and there are open-source speech models. I never liked them, because they run on your own machine and take resources away from everything else. I wanted transcription in the cloud, quick and fast enough that I'd stop noticing it.

I knew OpenRouter had added a long list of transcription models behind one API. So I ran an experiment: take Claude, take the Wispr Flow app installed on my Mac, decompile it to understand how it actually works, then rebuild the parts I use every day.

It worked. I've been dictating this project into itself ever since.

I speak a mix of English, Hindi and Bengali, often in the same sentence. So I benchmarked the models on my own voice and picked the one that writes all of it back in Roman script, the way I'd actually type it. What you get here is the result: a native macOS app, about 4,000 lines of Swift, built in a day. Now it's yours.

How it works

One key. One API call.

Wispr Flow streams audio to its own servers, where one model transcribes and a second cleans up. FlowClone collapses both into a single request to an audio-capable model.

01

Hold Fn

A native Swift helper catches the key and reads the app you're in and the text before your cursor.

02

Speak

16 kHz mono audio from your mic, compressed to a small m4a file. Other audio is muted while you talk.

03

One call

OpenRouter → Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite transcribes, romanizes Hindi and Bengali, removes "um"s and applies self-corrections.

04

It types

Pasted at your cursor in any app, and your clipboard is restored. If the paste fails, the text stays on the clipboard.

What decompiling Wispr Flow taught me

  • It's an Electron app plus a native Swift helper for the hotkey, Accessibility and pasting.
  • Audio goes out as Opus over a two-way gRPC stream, TranscriptionService/TranscribeStream.
  • The secret sauce is context: cursor text, app, on-screen words and your dictionary all go to the formatter.

What FlowClone does differently

  • Pure native Swift and SwiftUI. No bundled Chromium, so it uses a fraction of the memory.
  • Cloud only, through your OpenRouter key. Swap in any of 25+ transcription models.
  • History, dictionary and snippets stay in plain files on your Mac.

Privacy

Your words stay yours.

No accounts, no analytics, no server of mine in the middle. Here's exactly what happens to your voice.

Where your audio goes

Your Mac→ OpenRouter→ Google Gemini (zero retention)→ back to your Mac my server

🔒 What's protected

  • Every request requires zero data retention: providers that don't store your audio or train on it.
  • History, recordings, dictionary and snippets stay in one folder on your Mac. Delete anytime in Settings.
  • No analytics or tracking. The only network calls are the transcription itself.
  • Password fields are never read. Sending cursor context can be switched off.

👀 If a friend invited you

  • You get your own key with a small spending cap, not a shared one.
  • Your friend can see how much credit you've used, and that's all. They never see what you said.
  • Your audio never passes through their computer or any server of theirs.
  • Want full control? Paste your own OpenRouter key in Settings anytime.

Don't take my word for it: the app is open source, and every network call lives in one file (Transcriber.swift).

FAQ

Good questions.

Is this Wispr Flow?+

No. FlowClone is an independent, unofficial experiment inspired by Wispr Flow. It isn't affiliated with or endorsed by Wispr, and it shares no code with Wispr Flow. If you want the polished product with mobile apps, a notetaker and support, go buy Wispr Flow. It's great.

Is it really free?+

The app is free and open source. Transcription runs on your own OpenRouter account, billed per second of audio, typically $0.30–$3 a month. You can see your running cost in the Hub.

Why cloud and not a local model?+

Local models are private and free per use, but they keep gigabytes in memory and hit your CPU or GPU every time you speak. FlowClone idles at about 34 MB and 0% CPU, and only uses the network while you're dictating. If you prefer local, the transcriber is a single file to swap out.

Where does my voice go?+

From your Mac to OpenRouter, which routes it only to providers with zero data retention (Google, for the default model). Nothing goes to me. History, recordings, your dictionary and snippets stay in ~/Library/Application Support/FlowClone. You can stop sending cursor context, or stop keeping audio, in Settings.

macOS says the app "can't be opened". What now?+

The download isn't notarized by Apple (that needs a paid developer account). The easiest fix is to install with the one-line Terminal command at the top of this page, which skips that warning. If you downloaded the zip, go to System Settings › Privacy & Security › Open Anyway. Or build it from source in two commands.

A friend sent me an invite. Who pays, and what can they see?+

Your friend gave you a personal key with a small credit cap (a few dollars covers weeks of daily use). They can see how much of that credit you've used, never your audio or text. When it runs out, add your own OpenRouter key in Settings and keep going.

Which languages work?+

The default prompt is tuned for English mixed with Hindi and Bengali, written back in Roman script. The underlying model handles dozens of languages. Edit the prompt (no rebuild needed) or swap the model to fit how you speak.

Windows? iPhone?+

Not yet. It's macOS only, and that's the fun part for whoever forks it next. The transcription side is a single HTTPS call, so the hard part of a port is the hotkey and the pasting.
